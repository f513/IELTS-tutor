I've helped thousands of Indian students get the IELTS scores they need. I work with them every day and there are some common issues that come up again and again.

I like to try and spot patterns when I'm teaching IELTS and there are some definite commonalities that emerge when working with Indian students.

Please note that not all Indian students are the same. You are all individuals and should prepare for your test in a way that takes your individuality into account.

However, there are some trends and reasons why and here they are:

1. Pronunciation Issues

Most Indian students have wonderful fluency in English. However, many of them can speak too quickly.

When this is combined with 'accent interference' it can cause difficulty for the listener.

2. Repetition

Many Indian schools seem to teach their students that you can never repeat a single word in the whole writing test.

They also tell their students that you cannot repeat ideas.

3. Using 'high-level' language

There also seems to be an emphasis on using complex language but not on accuracy.

4. Focusing on the trivial many, not the essential few

All students have this problem but I hear it from my Indian students more than most there.

There is a tendency to believe rumour and worry about things that have no effect on your score.
