## 1. Overview

35 videos. Duration split is sharply bimodal:

- **30 of 35 (86%) are short-form "#shorts"** clips, 16–60 seconds long (sum ≈1221s, mean ≈41s). These carry the "tips" subtype's real character: single-point hooks, usually a mistake→fix or weak-answer→strong-answer contrast, built for scroll-stopping rather than instruction.
- **5 of 35 (14%) are long-form videos** (11–49 minutes): "IELTS Speaking Tips You MUST Know Before Your Test" (11:23), "Top 20 IELTS Speaking Idioms with Band 9 Samples" (39:00), "The 3 Speaking Tricks That Get You Band 9" (18:36), "Get Band 9 After Using These Speaking Tips" (24:24), "Get Band 9 After Learning These Speaking Tips" (49:05). These are the only videos in the batch with substantive, multi-point structure (named frameworks, chaptered idiom lists, real-student case studies) — everything else is a single isolated hook.

**What kind of content this is:** this subtype is the channel's short-form/TikTok-style "quick hack" funnel. Nearly every short follows one of two templates: (a) a **before/after or weak/strong contrast** ("Band 5 vs. Band 9," "Correct vs. Wrong Grammar," "Simple vs. Advanced Words") shown side-by-side with no explanation of *how* to get from one to the other beyond the implied swap, or (b) a **single actionable micro-tip** stated as a title ("Avoid Repeating Words," "Ask Your Examiner Questions," "Extend Your Answers"). Several descriptions are near word-for-word restatements of the title with zero added specifics — these function purely as hooks into the channel's paid VIP Course or free PDF lead-magnets (most of the long-form videos, and several shorts, link to a "VIP Course 10% off," a "Band 9 Most Used Words workbook," a "500+ practice questions PDF," or an idioms/phrases PDF).

## 6. Agent brief contribution

Bullets a Speaking specialist agent can draw on from this batch, each flagged for confidence:

1. **[Channel-sourced, high confidence]** Push for extended, developed answers over short ones — this is the single most repeated message across the tips batch (7 separate shorts hammer "don't give short answers," "develop one idea fully instead of listing many," "say more, not less"). This is a safe, well-evidenced core recommendation to give users.
2. **[Channel-sourced, medium confidence]** Teach answers with a direct-answer-first structure: answer the question plainly, then add supporting detail/explanation — stated explicitly in "Answer Directly in IELTS Speaking for Better Scores!" (q070U5-dgoU).
3. **[Channel-sourced, medium confidence]** Avoid repeating the same word within an answer — reword or use a synonym, since repetition signals a limited vocabulary to the examiner — from the word-repetition pair of shorts (mCCjgQnPA3k, Sf139dNs8IU).
