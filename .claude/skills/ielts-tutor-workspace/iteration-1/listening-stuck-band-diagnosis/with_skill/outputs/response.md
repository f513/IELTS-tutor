Here's the honest answer: 15 practice tests with no movement almost never means you need test #16. It means you've been doing the wrong *kind* of practice — and that's not a guess, it's the exact trap the IELTS Advantage channel calls out by name: "the problem isn't effort, it's not knowing why your answers are wrong." Grinding full tests without diagnosing them is explicitly the thing that *doesn't* fix a stuck score, no matter how many you stack up.

So before you take test #16, I want you to go back through test #11 to #15 (or however many you still have the answer keys/scripts for) and actually do the diagnostic work you've probably been skipping:

**Step 1 — pull the audio script, not just the answer key.** For every question you got wrong, don't just mark it wrong — go find the exact sentence in the transcript where the answer was said.

**Step 2 — sort every wrong answer into one of four buckets, honestly:**
1. **Spelling** — you heard/understood the right word but wrote it wrong. (Be strict here: a misspelling is wrong, full stop, not "basically right.")
2. **Question-type weakness** — a specific format keeps beating you (maps, multiple choice, matching, whatever it is), not listening in general.
3. **Focus lapse** — you understood everything leading up to it, then blanked or drifted, usually late in a part.
4. **Vocabulary gap** — you literally didn't know the word that was said, even once you read the transcript.

**Step 3 — count them up.** Whichever bucket has the most entries across those 5 tests is your actual bottleneck, not "listening in general." That's what changes your prep, not another cold test.

A few things worth checking while you do that, based on where Band 6.5 plateaus usually live:

- **Where in the test are the wrong answers clustering?** If most of them are in Parts 3–4 and you're basically clean on Parts 1–2, that's completely normal and actually useful: Parts 3–4 are deliberately the hardest, and that gap is specifically what separates Band 7 from 8/9. If that's your pattern, the fix is weighting your *outside-the-test* listening practice toward that difficulty — multi-speaker debate-style podcasts for Part 3 style, TED-Talk-style single-speaker academic lectures for Part 4 style (listen for staging language like "firstly... then...") — not just more mock tests.
- **Is spelling quietly costing you more than you think?** This is the single most repeated mistake callers-out across this channel's material. It's an easy thing to undercount when you're grading yourself, because "I knew the word" feels like it should count. It doesn't, on the real test.
- **Are you replaying/pausing in practice?** If yes, that's probably inflating your practice scores relative to what you'll actually get on test day with single playback only — worth checking whether your "6.5 in practice" is even a real 6.5 under true conditions.
- **Are you using official material?** Up to 90% of free "IELTS listening practice" online is unreliable — too easy, too hard, or using question formats that don't appear on the real test. Since you said Cambridge, you're already doing the right thing there — don't switch to random YouTube/app practice chasing variety.

One more diagnostic question, because the fix really does branch depending on the answer: **when you miss a question, is it because you froze and lost the next 2-3 answers too, or is it one clean miss and you're fine for what follows?** If it's the former, that's a focus/stamina issue, not a comprehension one, and the fix is a progressive listening-stamina drill (5 minutes of focused listening, building up by a few minutes every day or two toward a full 30 minutes) rather than more vocabulary or more tests.
