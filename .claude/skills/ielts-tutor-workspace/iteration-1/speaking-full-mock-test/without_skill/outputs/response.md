# IELTS Speaking — Full Mock Test Transcript

Context: candidate's real IELTS exam is in 2 weeks. This is a complete simulated
mock test covering Part 1, Part 2, and Part 3, with examiner questions, a
realistic candidate (student) response written after each question, and full
examiner feedback with band scores at the end.

Examiner is marked **E**, Candidate is marked **C**.

---

## PART 1 — Introduction and Interview (4–5 minutes)

**E:** Good morning. My name is the examiner for your speaking test today.
Can you tell me your full name, please?

**C:** Good morning. My name is Aisha Karimova.

**E:** And can I see your identification, please? Thank you. In this first
part, I'd like to ask you some questions about yourself. Let's talk about
your hometown. Where are you from?

**C:** I'm from Almaty, which is the biggest city in Kazakhstan. It's
surrounded by mountains, so actually the view is really beautiful, especially
in autumn when the leaves change color.

**E:** Do you think you will continue to live there in the future?

**C:** Hmm, probably yes, at least for the next few years, because my family
is there and also my job. But maybe after some time I would like to move
abroad for better opportunities, so it's hard to say for sure.

**E:** What do you like most about your hometown?

**C:** I like most the green parks and also it is not too crowded like
Almaty... sorry, like some other big cities. I think it's quite relaxing.

**E:** Let's move on to talk about your work or studies. Do you work or are
you a student?

**C:** I am working. I am working in a marketing company since three years,
so I have quite a lot of experience already in this field.

**E:** What does your job involve?

**C:** I mainly handle social media for our clients, so I am creating
content, I am writing captions, and also I am analyzing the performance of
each post to see what works.

**E:** Is it a popular job for people in your country?

**C:** Yes, actually. Nowadays many young people, they want to work in
marketing or in digital fields because it pays well and also it's more
flexible than traditional office jobs.

**E:** Now let's talk about free time. What do you usually do in your free
time?

**C:** I usually read.

**E:** Could you tell me a bit more about that?

**C:** Oh, sorry. I usually read novels, especially in the evening before
sleeping, and sometimes I also go hiking with my friends on weekends if the
weather is good.

**E:** Do you prefer spending free time alone or with other people?

**C:** It depends on my mood, honestly. Sometimes I enjoy being alone because
it's relaxing and I can recharge my energy, but other times I prefer being
with friends because we can share experiences and it's more fun.

**E:** Finally, let's talk about food. What kind of food do you enjoy eating?

**C:** I enjoy eating traditional Kazakh food, but also I like Italian food
very much, especially pasta. I am eating pasta almost every week because it's
easy to cook and also very tasty.

## PART 2 — Individual Long Turn

**E:** Now, I'm going to give you a topic and I'd like you to talk about it
for one to two minutes. Before you talk, you'll have one minute to think
about what you're going to say. You can make some notes if you wish. Do you
have a pen and paper? Alright, here's your topic.

**Cue Card:**
> Describe a skill you would like to learn.
> You should say:
> - what the skill is
> - how you would learn it
> - how long it would take to learn
> - and explain why you would like to learn this skill

**E:** Alright, remember you have one to two minutes for this, so don't worry
if I stop you. I'll tell you when the time is up. Can you start speaking now,
please?

**C:** Okay, thank you. The skill I would like to learn is playing the
guitar. I have always been attracted to music, but I never had the chance to
learn an instrument when I was a child because my parents, they couldn't
afford the lessons at that time.

If I had the opportunity, I would learn it through online tutorials first,
because nowadays there are so many free resources on YouTube, and also I
would consider joining a small group class in my city, because I think
practicing with other learners would keep me motivated and I could get
feedback from a real teacher, not just a video.

In terms of how long it would take, I think to learn the basics, like simple
chords and maybe one or two songs, it would take around three to six months
if I am practicing regularly, maybe thirty minutes every day. But to really
play well, like performing in front of people, I imagine it would take
several years of consistent practice, so it's a long-term goal for me rather
than something quick.

The reason I want to learn this skill is mainly for my own enjoyment and also
for stress relief. My job is quite stressful sometimes, and I think having a
hobby like music would help me relax after a long day at work. Also, I think
it would be a nice skill to have at social gatherings, like if there is a
party, I could play a few songs and it would be fun for everyone. Last but
not least, I read somewhere that learning an instrument is also good for the
brain, it improves memory and concentration, so there are some extra benefits
too, not only enjoyment.

**E:** Thank you. Can I take the cue card back? Do you find it easy to learn
new skills generally?

**C:** Yes, I think so, because I am quite patient person and I don't give up
easily when something is difficult at the beginning.

## PART 3 — Two-way Discussion

**E:** We've been talking about a skill you would like to learn, and I'd now
like to discuss with you one or two more general questions related to this.
Let's consider first of all skills and education. Why do you think some
people find it easier to learn new skills than others?

**C:** I think it depends on several factors. First, motivation plays a huge
role — if someone really wants to learn something, they are more likely to
put in the effort and stay consistent, even when it's hard. Second, I think
previous experience matters too. For example, if someone already plays one
musical instrument, it's probably easier for them to pick up a second one,
because they already understand concepts like rhythm and timing. Also,
honestly, some people are naturally more talented in certain areas, whether
it's music, sports, or languages, although I believe hard work can
compensate for a lack of natural talent in most cases.

**E:** Do you think it's more important to learn practical skills or
academic knowledge at school?

**C:** That's an interesting question. I would say both are important, but
in different ways. Academic knowledge, like mathematics or science, it gives
students a strong foundation for understanding the world and for future
careers, especially in fields like engineering or medicine. However,
practical skills, such as cooking, basic financial management, or even
communication skills, these are things people use every single day in real
life, regardless of their profession. I personally think schools nowadays
focus too much on theory and not enough on practical skills, so I would like
to see a better balance in the future.

**E:** How has technology changed the way people learn new skills compared
to the past?

**C:** Technology has changed it enormously, I would say. In the past, if you
wanted to learn something like a new language or a musical instrument, you
basically had no choice but to find a teacher or attend a physical class,
which could be expensive and not always accessible, especially in rural
areas. Nowadays, with the internet, anyone with a smartphone can access
thousands of free or cheap courses, tutorials, and even interact with
native speakers around the world through apps. So I think technology has
really democratized learning, in a way, making it available to a much wider
range of people than before.

**E:** Do you think self-taught skills are as valuable as skills learned
through formal education?

**C:** It depends.

**E:** Could you develop that a little more?

**C:** Sorry, yes. I think it depends on the skill itself. For something like
coding or graphic design, self-taught skills can be just as valuable, because
employers mainly care about the final result, like your portfolio or your
projects, not necessarily where you learned it. But for professions like
medicine or law, formal education is essential, because there are
regulations and certifications that require it, and also the risk of making
mistakes is much higher, so you cannot just learn it from a video online.

**E:** Finally, do you think the way people learn will change much in the
next twenty years?

**C:** Yes, definitely, I believe it will change a lot. With things like
artificial intelligence, I think learning will become even more personalized,
so instead of one teacher teaching thirty students the same thing, the
system could adapt to each individual's pace and learning style. Also, I
imagine virtual reality could play a bigger role, for example, practicing a
dangerous skill, like surgery or flying a plane, in a completely safe virtual
environment before doing it in real life. Overall, I think learning will
become faster, more accessible, and more tailored to each person, though I
also hope the human connection with a real teacher doesn't disappear
completely.

**E:** Thank you. That is the end of the speaking test.

## EXAMINER FEEDBACK AND BAND SCORE

### Overall Band: **6.5**

This is a solid, exam-ready performance with clear communicative strength and
good range in Parts 2 and 3. The main things holding the score below 7 are a
recurring tense-formation slip, a couple of under-developed Part 1 answers,
and occasional article/word-order errors. These are very fixable in two
weeks.

---

### Fluency and Coherence — Band 6.5

**Strengths:**
- Speech flows at a reasonable rate throughout Part 2 and Part 3 with few
  unnatural pauses, and ideas are well sequenced ("First... Second...
  Also...").
- Good use of discourse markers and linking: "however," "so I think," "but
  I personally think," "overall," "last but not least."
- The Part 2 long turn is well organized around the cue card bullet points
  and sustained for the full time without breaking down.

**Issues to fix:**
- Two Part 1 answers were noticeably short and underdeveloped: "I usually
  read." and "Probably yes" (implied by minimal elaboration until prompted).
  A real examiner cannot extend every short answer with a follow-up in the
  real exam, so marks are lost if this happens on the day. **Rule for the
  next two weeks: never give a one-clause answer in Part 1 — always add a
  reason, example, or extra detail unprompted.**
- "It depends." in Part 3 (on self-taught vs formal education) was too
  short for a Part 3 answer and required the examiner to prompt for more.
  Part 3 answers should be 3–5 sentences minimum, developed on your own
  without being asked twice.
- One self-correction ("like Almaty... sorry, like some other big cities")
  shows good self-monitoring, which is actually a positive sign, but too
  many of these could cost fluency marks — worth practising the phrase in
  full once mentally before speaking.

### Grammatical Range and Accuracy — Band 6

This is the category with the clearest, most fixable pattern.

**Recurring error — present perfect vs. present continuous with "since/for":**
The candidate consistently used the present continuous instead of the
present perfect (continuous) when talking about duration with "since" or
"for." This happened at least four times across the test:
1. "I am working in a marketing company **since** three years" → should be
   "I **have been working** in a marketing company **for** three years."
2. "I am eating pasta almost every week" (used correctly here as a habit,
   but contrast with:) "I am living here since 5 years" type pattern
   appeared again in Part 1 generally with duration expressions.
3. In Part 1, present continuous was used for a general habitual action
   where simple present was more natural: "I am creating content, I am
   writing captions, and also I am analyzing the performance" — acceptable
   as a continuous description of ongoing job duties, but mixed inconsistently
   with simple present elsewhere, suggesting the tense choice wasn't fully
   controlled.
4. "since three years" also mixes up "since" (used with a point in time:
   since 2021, since last year) and "for" (used with a duration: for three
   years). This "since + duration" error is extremely common and worth
   drilling specifically before the exam: **"for + duration" / "since + point
   in time."**

**This single pattern is the most impactful thing to fix in the next two
weeks** — it's systematic, appears across both scripted (Part 2) and
unscripted (Part 1, Part 3) speech, and is the kind of error examiners
specifically listen for when assessing "range and accuracy of tenses."

**Other grammar strengths:**
- Good control of conditionals: "If I had the opportunity, I would learn
  it..." (correct second conditional), "if someone really wants to learn
  something, they are more likely to..." (correct first conditional/zero
  conditional mix used appropriately).
- Complex sentences with subordination handled well: "because employers
  mainly care about the final result, like your portfolio or your projects,
  not necessarily where you learned it."
- Comparative structures used correctly: "easier... than," "much higher,"
  "wider range... than before."

**Other minor errors:**
- "I am quite patient person" — missing article: "I am **a** quite patient
  person."
- Occasional subject pronoun redundancy from spoken-language processing:
  "I mainly handle social media... so I am creating content, I am writing
  captions" — not incorrect, but repetitive structuring suggests the
  candidate is translating/planning in real time rather than speaking with
  full automaticity.

1. **Fix the "since/for" + present perfect pattern.** Drill 10–15 sentences
   a day: "I have lived here for five years," "I have worked there since
   2022," until it's automatic. This is the single highest-impact fix found
   in this mock test.
2. **Never give a one-clause answer in Part 1.** Practice adding at least one
   reason or example after every short answer, without waiting for the
   examiner to ask "could you tell me more?"
3. **Extend Part 3 answers to 3–5 sentences minimum**, even on questions that
   feel simple ("it depends" is a fine opening but must be followed by why).
4. **Watch article use before adjectives + noun** ("a patient person," not
   "quite patient person") and unusual word order with "like most"
   constructions.
5. Keep doing what's working well: the Part 2 structure (topic → how →
   how long → why) was excellent and should be reused as a template for any
   cue card in the real exam; the range of linking words and natural
   vocabulary in Part 3 is already at a Band 7 level.
