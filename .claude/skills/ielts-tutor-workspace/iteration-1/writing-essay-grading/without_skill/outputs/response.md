## Criterion-by-criterion breakdown

### Task Response — Band 6
- The task is a "to what extent do you agree or disagree" question. You've chosen a valid and common approach: discuss both sides, then land on a clear, consistent partial-agreement position in the introduction and conclusion. The position doesn't waver, which examiners reward.
- However, each body paragraph only lists two examples (communication / online shopping-banking in favour of "simpler"; new skills / notifications in favour of "complicated") without really developing *why* these matter. For example, "online shopping and banking has made daily tasks much more convenient" states a claim but doesn't extend it with a concrete explanation or result.
- The response is relevant throughout and ideas are adequately addressed, but development is somewhat mechanical — more "list and briefly illustrate" than "explain and extend." This, plus the slightly short length, caps it at Band 6 rather than 7.

### Coherence and Cohesion — Band 6
- Clear four-paragraph structure (intro / for-benefits / for-complications / conclusion) that's easy to follow.
- Good range of cohesive devices: "On the one hand," "For example," "In addition," "On the other hand," "For instance," "Moreover," "In conclusion," "Therefore." This is a strength.
- But the linking is somewhat formulaic — a fairly mechanical list of linkers rather than cohesion built through varied referencing and substitution. One reference is a little unclear: "...save a lot of time for people, **which** they can use for other important things" — "which" refers awkwardly back to "time," and the clause reads slightly clumsily.
- Paragraphing is logical, but within paragraphs the two examples in each aren't linked to each other — they sit side by side rather than building on one another.

### Lexical Resource — Band 6
- Topic-appropriate vocabulary is present (smartphones, internet, online shopping and banking, notifications, mental health, stressful for older generations), showing adequate range for the topic.
- Noticeable, somewhat frequent errors in spelling and word choice reduce the score:
  - "state**ment**" → *statment*
  - "comm**u**nicate" → *comunicate*
  - "th**ei**r" → *thier*
  - "stress**ful**" → *stressfull*
  - "d**i**fficult" → *dificult*
  - "**affect** their mental health" → *effect* (classic affect/effect confusion)
  - "more **easier**" → double comparative error (should be simply "easier")
  - Several singular/plural slips: "a lot of **benefit**" → benefits; "both **point** of view" → points of view; "some **aspect**" → aspects; "new **complication**" → complications; "**constant notification**" → notifications
- These errors are frequent enough to be noticeable and occasionally distracting, but meaning is never lost — consistent with Band 6 ("uses an adequate range of vocabulary... but makes noticeable errors in spelling and/or word formation that may cause some difficulty for the reader").

### Grammatical Range and Accuracy — Band 6
- You do attempt some complex structures — relative clauses ("which they can use for..."; "who did not grow up with these devices"), and a reasonable mix of simple and complex sentences.
- But subject-verb agreement errors recur:
  - "online shopping and banking **has** made" → compound subject needs "have"
  - "technology also **bring** some complication" → "brings"
  - "it has also **introduce** new complication" → "introduced" (past participle needed after "has")
- Article/number errors: "stressful for **older generation**" → "older generations" / "the older generation"
- These errors are frequent but do not seriously impede communication — the reader can follow your meaning throughout, which is exactly the Band 6 descriptor ("makes some errors in grammar... but they rarely reduce communication").

This is a solid, clearly-organised Band 6 essay: a consistent, relevant position, logical structure, and good use of basic cohesive devices, held back by frequent spelling slips (statment, comunicate, thier, stressfull, dificult), a recurring affect/effect confusion, several singular/plural and subject-verb agreement errors, and body paragraphs that list ideas rather than developing them in depth.

## Top 3 things to fix for a quick band boost
1. **Proofread for spelling/grammar before finishing** — statment, comunicate, thier, stressfull, dificult, and affect/effect are all errors a careful re-read would catch, and fixing them alone would lift Lexical Resource and GRA.
2. **Develop each example further** — for every point, add one more sentence explaining the mechanism or consequence (e.g., *why* constant notifications hurt mental health, with a specific effect such as poorer sleep or reduced concentration at work) rather than moving straight to the next point.
3. **Watch subject-verb agreement with compound subjects** ("shopping and banking **have**...", "technology...**brings**...") and plural nouns after "some/many/both" (benefits, complications, points of view, aspects).
