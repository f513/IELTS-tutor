## 1. Task Response — Band 6

- You take a clear, consistent position ("I partially agree") and your **conclusion's stance matches your introduction's** ("Therefore I partially agree that technology has made life more complicated") — that's a concrete thing the channel checks for, and you got it right.
- The essay addresses both sides of the debate, which is a reasonable way to answer a "to what extent" question.
- The main gap: each body paragraph tries to cover **two separate ideas** (Para 2: communication *and* online shopping/banking; Para 3: having to learn new skills *and* notifications/mental health) and only gives each a sentence or two. The channel's model for a body paragraph is **topic sentence → explanation → one example**, developed properly — not two ideas skimmed. This is a mild version of what the channel calls the "Shopping List" pattern: more ideas, less depth. One well-explained idea per paragraph, with a real example (a specific person, situation, or scenario — "my grandmother struggled to switch to using a smartphone for banking" rather than a generic statement) would raise this.
- **Word count: ~238 words — under the 250-word floor.** The channel treats 250 as a hard floor tied directly to Task Response, not a cosmetic target. This alone caps the score below where the ideas and control might otherwise land it.

## 2. Coherence & Cohesion — Band 6.5

- Paragraphing is logical and does what it should: intro → benefits → drawbacks → conclusion.
- Linking devices ("On the one hand," "On the other hand," "For example," "In addition," "For instance," "Moreover," "In conclusion," "Therefore") are used **purposefully, not maximally** — exactly what the channel wants. Band 9 cohesion "attracts no attention"; you're not overloading sentences with linking words or opening every sentence with one, which is good and worth noting explicitly, since over-linking is a common self-inflicted problem the channel calls out.
- Where it loses a bit: because two ideas are packed into each body paragraph, the internal logic occasionally reads as a list ("In addition... Moreover...") rather than one idea being built on and extended, which is the "faulty or mechanical" cohesion Band 6 describes at its lower edge.

## 3. Lexical Resource — Band 6

- Genuinely good news: there is **no sign of "Peacock" vocabulary-chasing** — no risky C1/C2 words thrown in to sound advanced. Your word choices ("benefit," "convenient," "complication," "constantly," "notification," "mental health") are simple, controlled, and almost all used correctly. That's the channel's "100% Rule" and "simplicity beats sophistication" philosophy working in your favor — this is a strength, not a weakness, and I want to confirm it explicitly rather than just hunting for errors.
- What caps it at 6 rather than 7 is **spelling, not word choice**: *statment, comunicate, thier* (×2), *stressfull, dificult* — five spelling slips. Band 6 Lexical Resource explicitly allows "some errors in spelling... but they do not impede communication," which fits here; Band 7 needs spelling errors to be rare, which this doesn't quite reach.
- One real word-choice (not spelling) error: **"can effect their mental health negatively" → should be "affect."** This is a classic mix-up (effect = noun/to bring about; affect = to influence) worth fixing specifically.

## 4. Grammatical Range and Accuracy — Band 5.5–6

The channel's own framing: more than 50% of your sentences need to be **completely error-free** to reach Band 7+. Going sentence-by-sentence, only about 3 of 11 sentences are fully clean — so this sits below that threshold, even though none of the errors are severe enough to confuse a reader. You do attempt some complex structures (relative clauses: "which they can use for...", "who did not grow up with...", "that did not exist before"), which is good — the channel's own ex-examiners say range isn't the real issue, accuracy is, and that's exactly what's happening here.

Recurring error types (fix these as a cluster, not one at a time — that's the channel's "diagnose the actual pattern" approach):

- **Subject–verb agreement**: "technology also **bring**" → *brings*; "online shopping and banking **has** made... and **save**" → *have made... and have saved* (compound subject needs a plural verb, and the verb tense should stay consistent across the sentence).
- **Double comparative**: "made life **more easier**" → *easier*.
- **Verb form after "has"**: "it has also **introduce**" → *introduced* (present perfect needs the past participle).
- **Missing plurals**: "both **point of view**" → *points of view*; "some **aspect** of life" → *aspects*.
- **Article/number with "generation"**: "stressful for **older generation**" → *the older generation* or *older generations*.

## Concrete rewrites

> Some people think that it has made life **more easier** and convenient
→ Some people think that it has made life **easier** and more convenient.

> I will discuss **both point of view** in this essay.
→ I will discuss **both points of view** in this essay. *(Minor style note: this sentence is functionally an outline/signpost line. It's not the severe "memorized hook/thesis opener" violation the channel ranks worst — because your actual position, "partially agree," is clearly stated right next to it — but a tighter version would fold the plan into the position itself rather than announcing it separately, e.g. "In my opinion, while technology has simplified many daily tasks, it has also added new complications.")*

> Online shopping and banking **has made** daily tasks much more convenient and **save** a lot of time for people, which they can use for other important things in **thier** life.
→ Online shopping and banking **have made** daily tasks much more convenient and **have saved** people a lot of time, which they can use for other important things in their lives.

> technology also **bring** some complication to modern life.
→ technology also **brings complications** to modern life.

> this can be very **stressfull** for **older generation** who did not grow up with these devices.
→ this can be very **stressful** for **the older generation**, who did not grow up with these devices.

> the constant notification from phones and social media can make it **dificult** for people to focus on their work and can **effect** their mental health negatively.
→ the constant notifications from phones and social media can make it **difficult** for people to focus on their work and can **affect** their mental health negatively.

> while technology has definitely made some **aspect** of life easier, I believe it has also **introduce** new **complication** that did not exist before.
→ while technology has definitely made some **aspects** of life easier, I believe it has also **introduced new complications** that did not exist before.

2. **One idea per body paragraph, fully developed with a real example.** Pick your strongest point in each paragraph (e.g., instant global communication; the older generation struggling with constant app/skill updates) and cut the second, underdeveloped idea, or move it to a second sentence of support for the same idea rather than a new one.
3. **Fix the five recurring grammar patterns above** (subject–verb agreement, plurals, verb forms) — these are mechanical and highly fixable with focused proofreading, not a sign of a deeper language gap. Per the channel's own logic, you don't need "more range" — you need to tighten what's already here.
4. **Proofread specifically for spelling** (statment, comunicate, thier, stressfull, dificult) — these are the kind of small, fixable slips the channel says can be the actual difference between a 6.5 and a 7 once everything else is in place.

Keep doing what you're already doing well: staying with simple, controlled, natural vocabulary instead of reaching for impressive-sounding words you're not fully sure of, and keeping your stance consistent from introduction to conclusion.
